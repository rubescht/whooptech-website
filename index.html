The voice gap

The people with the most to say are the hardest to hear online

Three in four First Nations people in remote and very remote communities are digitally excluded. Nearly half of remote First Nations communities have no mobile service. When you can't get online, you can't apply for the grant, answer the consultation, run the business or correct the record. Closing the Gap Target 17 set 2026 as the year digital inclusion would be equal. It isn't yet.

Sources: Mapping the Digital Gap 2025 Outcomes Report (RMIT, Swinburne, ADM+S, Telstra); Australian Digital Inclusion Index 2023. Read the summary.

On community terms

The Whoop Whoop AI Framework

A stronger voice only counts if it's still the community's voice. Every organisation adopts AI through six steps, led by its own people. Nothing starts without the Elders Council.

Consent and co-design

Elders, boards and community decide whether, where and how AI is used.

Data sovereignty

Community data stays owned and controlled by the community, following the Maiam nayri Wingara and CARE principles.

Cultural protection

Clear rules on what never goes into AI tools: sacred or restricted knowledge, images of people who have passed, stories without permission.

Safe tools

Approved tools only, privacy locked down, Australian hosting where possible, accounts managed by the organisation.

Policy and oversight

A plain-English AI policy for each organisation. People check AI outputs; AI never decides about individuals.

Review and improve

Regular check-ins with the community on what's working and any harms to fix.

Where the line is drawn

Some knowledge never leaves community. Everything else passes only through the Elders Council.

The training pathway

Delivered on Country where possible, in plain English or language, with Elders and local staff involved. The best graduates become Community AI champions, and the most capable are employed by us as trainers and technicians.

About us

Indigenous-owned, Territory-based, run by our own people

Whoop Whoop Technology is majority Aboriginal-owned and controlled, based in the Northern Territory. An Indigenous-majority board sets strategy and budget, an Elders Council guides cultural matters and must approve any community AI or data project, and an Aboriginal General Manager runs the business day to day. Our technicians, trainers and 24/7 support staff are Whoop Whoop Technology people, with a target of most field staff being Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander, trained and employed from the communities we serve.

Our commitments
